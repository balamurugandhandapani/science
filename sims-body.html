Physics Simulations

Move the sliders and watch the physics respond — a pendulum, double-slit interference, the photoelectric effect and Bragg diffraction. Every number on the screen is computed live from the equations shown.

Simple Pendulum Lab

Integrated numerically (4th-order Runge–Kutta), so large angles are exact — no small-angle approximation. The period does not depend on the mass, but the energy does. The exact period uses the arithmetic–geometric mean (AGM); for small angles it approaches . Damping makes the swing die away.

Double-Slit Interference

The fast cosine fringes come from the two slits (); the slow envelope comes from the width of each slit (). Make the slits narrower and the envelope widens; move them further apart and the fringes get closer. Far-field (Fraunhofer) model with the screen distance .

Photoelectric Effect

Below the threshold frequency no electrons come out however bright the light; above it, brightness changes how many (the current), while the colour sets their maximum energy. A negative collector voltage repels electrons until none arrive at . The current curve is an idealised straight-line model. Work functions are typical polycrystalline values (HyperPhysics, from the CRC Handbook) and vary by about 5–10% with surface condition.

Bragg Diffraction

Rays reflecting from neighbouring planes travel an extra path ; when that equals a whole number of wavelengths they add up and a strong peak appears. With more planes the peaks get sharper — the reason very small crystals give broad X-ray peaks (the Scherrer equation).
