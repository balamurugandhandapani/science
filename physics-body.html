Physics Tools

Seven quick calculators for physics and materials-science students — convert photon energy and wavelength, work out Bragg diffraction and Scherrer crystallite size, trace a projectile, solve Ohm’s law, convert units, and look up fundamental constants.

Photon Energy ↔ Wavelength ↔ Frequency

E = hc / λ = hν  ·  hc = 1239.842 eV·nm

Type any one quantity and choose its unit — all the others are calculated. For a semiconductor, enter the band gap in eV to get the absorption-edge wavelength. Constants use the exact SI (2019) values of h, c, e and NA. Region boundaries (e.g. visible = 380–750 nm) are conventional and differ slightly between sources.

Bragg’s Law (X-ray Diffraction)

nλ = 2d sinθ  ·  a = d√(h²+k²+l²) (cubic)

Enter (h k l) only if the material is cubic and you want the lattice parameter a. Q = 2π/d is the scattering vector. Preset wavelengths are the standard Bearden (1967) Kα lines; the “avg” entries are the Kα1:Kα2 = 2:1 intensity-weighted means used by NIST. Choose “Custom” or edit the wavelength box for any other source.

Scherrer Equation – Crystallite Size

D = Kλ / (β cosθ)  ·  βcorr = √(βobs² − βinst²)

β is the full width at half maximum of the diffraction peak, entered in degrees of 2θ (it is converted to radians internally). K ≈ 0.9 for spherical crystallites. Peak width also depends on strain and instrument, so the result is a lower bound on size; the equation is meaningful only for crystallites below roughly 100–200 nm. The correction assumes Gaussian peak shapes.

Projectile Motion

x = v₀cosα·t  ·  y = h₀ + v₀sinα·t − ½gt²

Ideal motion in a vacuum — air resistance is ignored, and the ground is flat at y = 0. Use g = 1.62 for the Moon or 3.73 for Mars (NASA mean surface gravity). Angles range from −90° (straight down) to 90° (straight up).

Ohm’s Law & Power

V = IR  ·  P = VI = I²R = V²/R

Resistor Combinations

Rseries = ΣR  ·  1/Rparallel = Σ(1/R)

Fill in exactly two of the four boxes (voltage, current, resistance, power) and leave the other two empty — the page calculates the rest. Use the resistor tool to find the equivalent resistance of any number of resistors.
